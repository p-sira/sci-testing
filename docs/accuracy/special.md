# special: library comparison

[All modules and methodology](README.md) · [Public API](https://docs.rs/sci-special)

Measured binary64 (`f64`) results using **Sci-special as the reference**.
Each library is compared with Sci on the same inputs. These differences measure
agreement with Sci; independent accuracy references remain in the raw CSVs. This page
covers 811236 cases and 405 public API names;
coverage is limited to the operations and inputs below.

## Agreement with Sci

`Sci vs reference` is **maximum / mean difference between Sci and the saved
independent mathematical reference in ε**. Other library cells are **maximum /
mean difference from Sci in ε** over cases
where both evaluations succeed. `Cases` counts input cases; `Sci failed N/T`
identifies missing baseline evaluations. `failed N/T` counts that library's own
failures; `Sci unavailable N/T` counts its successful evaluations excluded
because Sci failed. `unavailable N/T` means the library has no compatible
public operation for those inputs or distribution families; they are excluded
from the reported mean and are not counted as numerical failures. A dash means
no saved comparison or no paired finite results.
Means include all compared scalar components, including exact zeros.
Full outputs, references, warnings and errors remain in the per-function CSVs.
Tables are grouped by Sci 0.3 public submodule using the compiler-resolved
canonical API inventory. Historical flat recipe aliases appear under their
current public namespace, with their measured recipe names preserved.

## Conventions and limitations

All public special-function implementations have typed registrations or an explicit policy recipe. Original erf/gamma recipes use high-precision mpmath; added recipes use independent public SciPy calls, identities or quadrature. Polynomial constructors return descending coefficients; sequence and tuple ordering follows the pinned fixtures. Boost.Math/GSL are called where the bridge has matching scalar APIs, converting elliptic parameter m to modulus sqrt(m). Public GSL provides complex digamma and complex Spence; its Spence on negative real inputs with negative imaginary zero selects the upper bank, which can disagree with the independent lower-bank reference. Additional Bessel comparisons are explicitly derived: real-positive-argument Hankel functions combine public cylindrical J/Y functions, zero collections use public Bessel/Airy zeros and bracketed derivative roots, and Riccati arrays use spherical Bessel recurrences. Modified-Bessel I ratios use Boost I evaluations for moderate arguments, plus independent small-argument series and large-argument Riccati asymptotics where a direct Boost quotient would underflow or overflow; the latter cases are not direct Boost evaluations. Bessel integrals use Boost Gauss–Kronrod quadrature over either Boost or GSL Bessel kernels: GSL-labelled integral results therefore share a Boost quadrature backend, not an independent GSL integration algorithm. These composed bridges enforce bounded real domains. Kelvin ber/bei, real Struve and the sampled half-order Wright-Bessel functions use public Boost.Math generalized hypergeometric pFq reductions, with their own narrower domains. The three real Struve-integral comparisons similarly use public Boost 2F3 identities. Kelvin ker/kei and derivative comparisons and the full Kelvin tuple use GSL quadrature of defining complex-Bessel integral representations, not a public complex-valued GSL Bessel function. None of these adapters implies support for general complex-argument Bessel functions. GSL-labelled elementary information functions and trigonometric degree conversions are similarly composed from its public real special-function primitives, with independently validated input restrictions. Further native competitors are independently validated compositions, not a claim of matching named APIs: GSL fixed Gaussian quadrature provides nodes and weights for Legendre, Jacobi, Gegenbauer, Laguerre, Hermite and Chebyshev roots, with explicit shifted/scaled rule transformations. Boost Legendre roots and Q primitives produce the sampled roots_legendre/lqn arrays. Public GSL Airy zero routines supply both zeros and derivative zeros and their companion values; GSL Bessel Jn/Yn primitives and root solvers give integer-order zero collections, with full sequence ordering validated against the independent SciPy fixtures. Additional scalar inverses, gamma/elliptic identities, Box-Cox/logistic transforms, and combinatorial functions compose Boost.Math or GSL primitives under guarded domains. GSL Kelvin ber/bei and derivatives reuse the already documented GSL quadrature backend; they are not public GSL Kelvin APIs. Public mpmath numerical functions provide additional comparisons where the saved Sci inputs admit an equivalent real or complex convention. Some original mathematical references were also produced with mpmath; those rows therefore share a numerical backend and must not be treated as two independent confirmations. The saved full-precision reference remains distinct from the binary64 comparison output.

Inputs are generated deterministically into `sci-testing/cases/generated/<module>/<function>.csv` (optionally `.gz`); raw outputs, high-precision
references and the environment are stored in that repository's `results/`.
NaN, infinity, signed-zero contracts, invalid domains and work-limit enforcement
are outside this finite-input accuracy sample. Existing Sci API tests cover
additional behavior. The [coverage audit](../../results/coverage.csv) checks every public function
against the compiler-resolved inventory. These samples do not establish
worst-case bounds or full domain coverage. SciPy aliases, compatibility namespaces and Cython
variants are not counted as independent implementations.
